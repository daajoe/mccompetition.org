### Motivation

Propositional model counting (#SAT or number SAT) asks for the number of satisfying assignments (models) of a
propositional formula. #SAT is canonical for the complexity class #P. Counting solutions can solve any problem in the
Polynomial Hierarchy (PH) due to Toda’s Theorem.

#SAT remains computationally hard on many classes of formulas, while SAT (deciding if at least one solution exists) can
be easy for some, like 2-CNFs. Despite the hardness, efficient solvers now solve larger problems and have immediate use
in areas such as quantitative formal verification of hardware, software, and security protocols, combinatorial
optimization, computational mathematics, and quantum circuit simulation. Recent applications also impact probabilistic
reasoning, neuro-symbolic learning, and computational biology. An annual community event since 2020 evaluates solvers
and provides a snapshot of the state of the art.

Modern #SAT solvers use diverse and sophisticated techniques beyond simple enumeration, with connections between
structure and algorithms. Approximate approaches have recently gained attention, while practical improvements continue.
Beyond counting, knowledge compilation is important for knowledge representation, constraint satisfaction, algorithms,
complexity theory, machine learning, and databases.

Verifiable solving helps to identify and avoid bugs by emitting proof traces or showing formula equivalence, which can
be mathematically checked and audited. Efforts for trustworthy solvers also inspire new proof systems. Proofs and
solvers are linked: proof systems model solving, help analyze solver limits, and enable certification of advanced
techniques.

The need for efficient, reliable solvers and robust theoretical understanding is rising as problems grow more complex.
Despite progress, research communities rarely interact on quantitative aspects, leaving much potential untapped. This
seminar aims to connect these areas and foster collaboration between theory and practice.
